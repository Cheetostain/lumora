Where to find your numbers

  1. Jobs never billedYour job list beside your invoices for the last 3 months, in QuickBooks or your job app.
  2. Quotes that went coldYour quote list, sorted by status.
  3. Typing the same job twiceA stopwatch on one ordinary day.
  4. Calls nobody called backYour phone's call history, on the Missed tab, or your business line's online account. How to get 30 days of call history

How we count

  1. Typing the same job twice. Minutes a day × workdays ÷ 60 × the hourly cost. With no hourly cost it stays as hours, out of the total.
  2. Jobs never billed. The count for 3 months ÷ 3 × your average invoice. The whole invoice, because the parts and the hours are already spent.
  3. Quotes that went cold. Quotes a month × the profit on a quoted job.
  4. Calls nobody called back. (New numbers that rang out − the ones you called back) × 52 weeks ÷ 12 months × the share that would have booked × the profit on a job. Our article shows how to count it from a month of call history.

The total adds the lines you filled in, rounded to the nearest ten dollars.

What this leaves out

  • Supplier bills that don't match what you ordered.
  • Supply-house tickets with no job number.
  • Reading emails and filing the attachments by hand.
  • Reminding your team and customers, one at a time.
  • Keeping every calendar up to date by hand.
  • Adding up the month-end numbers.

Where the example numbers come from

  • 24%: what trade shops with fewer than 5 technicians booked in a study of more than 3,000 trade businesses in the US and Canada; all of them booked 42% (ServiceTitan blog, 2022, vendor data).
  • $150 profit on a job: $341, HomeAdvisor's national average plumber visit (2026), × 44.3%, Level's median job gross margin (vendor data), rounded.
  • The other examples are round numbers, not data.
