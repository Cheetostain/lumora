Where the first fix will likely start

Most Gainesville leases end July 31. So a year of move-outs, inspections and deposit claims lands in about two weeks.

Often the first fix is a deposit clock for turnover week: every move-out, its inspection, its charges, its day-30 deadline, the day the tenant got the notice and the day any refund is due, on one list, with the claim letter drafted for you to check and send.

And vendor bills set up to come into AppFolio or Buildium matched to their work order, then billed to the owner or charged back to the tenant. The best time to fix turnover is before it, in the spring.

A one-job find

Damage bigger than the deposit. Within two miles of UF, a student lease backed by a parent guarantor carries a deposit of about $52 on average (UF Off-Campus Life, 2026-27).

When the damage runs to a few hundred dollars, the notice still has to go out by day 30 to keep the claim on the deposit, and the balance above it is billed to the tenant and the guarantor. The sheet counts move-outs where the charges ran past the deposit, and how much of that came in.

For a property manager, the price after the walk depends on how big the leak is, how many doors you manage and what your software can already do.

What we never build

  1. No automation sends a deposit claim on its own. A person checks every charge and sends the notice the way the law allows: certified mail, or email only where the tenant agreed in writing to get notices by email (F.S. 83.49 and 83.505).

  2. Late-rent reminders go from your line, in your name, and only to current tenants.

  3. We build inside what you already pay for. If a fix needs a new subscription, you hear the monthly cost before we start, and why the free option lost.

  4. Say no once, and that's the end of it. After a no, we don't call, text or stop by again unless you ask.
