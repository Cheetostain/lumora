Rules for your trade

Two more lines we count, and the rules we build around

  1. Estimates that go quiet. A retail estimate runs $8,000 to $15,000 on average nationally (Foundry CRO, 2026, vendor compilation), so one that goes quiet matters. Reminders go to you; one to the homeowner needs written consent.

  2. Dates nobody watched. In unincorporated Alachua County, a missed in-progress inspection means tearing roofing back off so the inspector can see the deck. A supplement filed more than 18 months after the storm is too late (F.S. 627.70132).

  3. No gifts for an inspection or a claim. No text, box or promotion offers a homeowner a rebate, gift card, cash, coupon, deductible waiver or any other thing of value for a roof inspection or an insurance claim, and no automation pays a referral reward on a job funded by insurance proceeds (F.S. 489.147, up to $10,000 a violation).

  4. The claim notice, on anything we help send. Any flyer, door hanger or text we help with that mentions a roof claim carries the required notice in 12-point type or larger: the homeowner pays the deductible, waiving it is insurance fraud, and so is filing a false claim.

General information, not legal advice.
