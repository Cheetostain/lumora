01

What the walk looks for

  • Calls after hours, peaking in summer

    In a national study of residential AC shops, 14.1 percent of calls came after hours in June, against 9.8 percent in October, and the busiest window was 5 to 9 p.m. (ServiceTitan, 2026, vendor data). Some larger shops in town already answer around the clock. The walk checks whether yours has a gap, after hours and at 2 p.m.

  • Replacement quotes that wait

    A new system is the biggest quote you write. Nationally, an AC install averages $5,978, with a typical range of $3,887 to $8,073 (HomeAdvisor, 2026, national cost guide). The sheet counts the days from visit to quote in your own export.

  • Maintenance nobody calls about

    The tune-up that is due, the customer from last spring. The sheet counts customers in your invoices with a job one to three years ago and none since. Texting them a reminder needs their written consent, so the fix may be a call list for you instead.

  • Texts in one language

    Some booking tools confirm every job in English, even for customers who called in Spanish. The sheet notes how many of your customers would rather read Spanish.

02

Where the first fix will likely start

Often one of two. The plan list: which members are owed a visit and whose renewal comes due, so no maintenance plan lapses unnoticed. Or the after-hours phone rule, set before summer. Both show up in your own records within weeks.

The sheet decides, not this page. We pick the leak that recovers the most for the effort, one you can watch move in your own records within 90 days.

03

The season

Summer is the test. In the national data above, the after-hours share of calls runs highest in June, so an after-hours fix is best set before the heat.
