What a walk looks for

  • Calls that ring out

    After five, on Saturdays and any time whoever answers the phone is under a sink. The sheet counts how many of those calls were never returned.

  • Quotes that leave late

    The visit was Tuesday. The quote went out Friday. By then the customer had two other quotes.

  • The same job, typed three times

    Once on the paper ticket, once in the calendar, once in QuickBooks. Nobody’s job, so it’s the owner’s job, at night.

  • Reviews never requested

    61 jobs finished this month, and nobody asked for a review. The Google page still shows 12 reviews.

  • Texts in one language

    The booking tool confirms every job in English, even for the customers who called in Spanish.

  • Follow-ups that never happen

    The maintenance that’s due, the estimate nobody answered, the customer from last spring. All real money, none of it on anyone’s list.

Rules we keep

It replaces retyping, waiting and forgetting.

Every fix goes after a task: the job typed into three places, the quote that waits until Friday, the follow-up nobody owns.

  • We build inside what you already pay for. If a fix needs a new subscription, you hear the monthly cost before we start, and why the free option lost.

  • No cameras. No recorded calls. Florida law needs everyone’s consent to record a call, and a crew should not work under a camera. We work from your call history, your invoices and your calendar.

  • No cold calls, no cold email. Work comes from people who pass our name along, and rooms we walk into.

One contact, start to finish

The person who walks your shop stays your contact, from the first conversation to the monthly check. No account managers, no handoffs.

In English or Spanish. Spanish is a first language here, not a translation.
